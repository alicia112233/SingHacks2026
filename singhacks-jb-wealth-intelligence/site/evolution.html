How it evolved

We built a smart assistant. We learned to build for a bank.

At SingHacks we asked one question: are the AI's answers good? We removed names before calling outside models and thought that was enough. Over the two months since, user testing with a Julius Bär RM training lead and our own research taught us to ask a different question first: where is client data allowed to go at all? This page shows how that changed the product.

At SingHacks we thought

“Strip the names, send the numbers to the best AI models, and let them tell the RM what will happen.”

→

Now we think

“Code calculates. AI reads only what it is allowed to. The RM decides.”

At SingHacks: to review each recommended action, TESSERA sent the client's portfolio numbers, with names removed, to three outside AI models. The red dot is client data leaving the bank. News went through keyword rules, and the RM was told what a headline would do.

Four shifts in how we think

1

Where client data is allowed to go

21 Oct and 30 Oct 2026

We believed

Removing names is enough. Outside AI models reviewed each action using the client's portfolio numbers, with names, IDs and RM notes stripped out.

What changed our mind

User testing

A Julius Bär RM training lead explained data classes. Client data (C3 to C4), anonymised or not, may only go to the bank's in-house AI, which runs offline. Tools like Copilot are cleared for public data (C1 to C2) only, and API use is restricted.

What we built

First, a stricter check that blocks any packet carrying a name, ID or note, plus a view of exactly what the model saw. Then a full split: outside models only read public headlines, and one in-house model reviews actions from three angles.

2

From predicting to screening

27 Oct 2026

We believed

A good alert tells the RM what a headline will do to a client's portfolio.

What changed our mind

User testing

The RM lead doubted any system can know every portfolio and predict each headline's impact. RMs already get market reviews every Monday and Thursday and a daily media review.

What we built

A “May be exposed” flag: which clients a headline touches and what to check. Scenarios are labelled as illustrations, and confidence is capped at 84 until a model is tested on real outcomes.

3

From hand-written rules to detection

20 Oct 2026

We believed

For a demo, it is fine to code each featured client's contradiction by hand.

What changed our mind

Our own review

A rule that names a client breaks on the next client. A bank has thousands, and judges would ask about client number 21.

What we built

Detection that reads any client's source of wealth, objectives and RM notes, and compares them with look-through holdings. No client is named in the rules.

4

Showing what is real

29 Oct 2026

We believed

A polished screen builds trust.

What changed our mind

User testing

In a bank, a screen that mixes real data with simulations, without saying which is which, loses trust. Pages must show what is simulated.

What we built

Every section carries a source tag: client data, calculation, illustration or public news. The demo's stand-in for the in-house model is labelled too.

What we want to learn next

Proposed

A 30-day shadow pilot with one desk

Measure how fast an RM reaches the first high-value review, how much evidence is complete, and why RMs override TESSERA.

Planned feature

China's offshore trust tax

Suggested by the RM lead as a real Singapore-relevant trigger: China's 20% tax on offshore trust income (MOF/STA Announcement No. 21 of 2026). Not built yet.
